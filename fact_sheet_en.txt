ABOUT
================================================================

Desktop Forge is an idle simulation that runs in a small window at the edge of your desktop
while you work. You watch autonomous artisans forge swords, hand those swords to the
adventurers who visit, and send them out into the world.

Every artisan has more than a set of stats: over 200 traits, plus their own specialties,
likes and dislikes. You never direct them move by move — you decide which jobs each of them
is allowed to take, and they choose when to gather, process, forge or rest. Who you put on
which stage shapes the flow of the workshop and the swords that come out of it. Everyday
interactions make moods rise and fall, and those moods show in the work and in the blades.

The materials used, the artisans involved, their moods and the quality of their work shape
how each sword splits between edge, durability and enchantment, and how well it is made. A
newly forged blade hides its true nature; appraisal uncovers it step by step.

Beyond your walls lie eight towns, the monster dens beside them, and three nations at war.
Markets, war and monsters move every day, whether or not you are watching: caravans carry
materials down the roads, a restless den thins out the loads, and fighting can cut a road so
no caravan comes at all. And the only thing that moves that world is, again, the sword you
forged. Every den your adventurers clear thins out the monsters; blades delivered to a
nation's commission become its equipment, push the front line, and can change which nation
rules a town.

Appraised swords go to the adventurers who visit your workshop. They are not your employees
— you cannot train them, command them, or bring them home. The only thing you control is the
sword in their hands. Every destination asks for a different shape of sword: a blade that
answers what the road demands carries its bearer through to the end and comes back as a log
full of coin and renown. An adventurer sent out with a blade that isn't good enough never
returns at all. What happened is written into your workshop's chronicle, and it stays there.

================================================================
KEY FEATURES
================================================================

- A desktop companion — a compact workshop that keeps moving in the corner of your screen
- Autonomous artisans — set the work policy and watch an idle workshop run itself
- Over 200 traits — specialties, likes, dislikes and the mood of the day shape the work
- A different sword every time — materials, artisans, emotions and luck show in its stats and quality
- A life on every blade — a weak sword breaks, its bearer never returns, and only the record remains
- A world that keeps moving — eight towns, monster dens and three warring nations move on without you
- Swords that change the world — clearing dens calms them; delivered blades push front lines and change who rules
- Long-term goals — codex records, a workshop expansion tree, a chronicle, and Steam leaderboards

================================================================
DEVELOPER
================================================================

Nulpoyo. A solo developer based in Japan, making games alongside a full-time office job.

I love idle games. Just having one running in the bottom-right corner of my screen makes
me want to keep working. I wanted to build that feeling itself.

I made death matter because it makes the sword you hand over matter. If the blade you
forged decides whether an adventurer comes home, you put more into forging it. And
picturing someone setting out with a zero-durability sword and going "oh — it broke"
halfway through the dungeon simply sounded fun to me.

I like games with a lot to chew on — which means mine tend to end up harder than I
intended. Most of the work on Desktop Forge has gone into balancing: keeping it something
you can leave running at the edge of your screen, while still leaving numbers worth
pushing on.

Find your own combination of smiths and forge the strongest blade you can. Then hand it
to an adventurer and see how deep they get.
