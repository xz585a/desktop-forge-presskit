概要

『Desktop Forge』は、作業中のデスクトップの片隅に置いたまま進行する、放置型の制作・経営シミュレーションです。自律的に動く職人たちを見守りながら剣を打ち、その剣を冒険者へ託し、世界へ送り出します。

工房の職人には、能力だけでなく200種類以上の個性と、得意分野、好き嫌いがあります。プレイヤーは一人ずつ指示を出すのではなく、任せる仕事を決めます。職人たちは自分で採集・加工・鍛造・休憩を選んで動き、誰をどの工程に置くかが、工房の流れと完成する剣を変えていきます。日々の交流で感情には波が生まれ、その気分は仕事の調子にも、出来上がる剣にも表れます。

投入した素材、担当した職人、そのときの感情や作業の出来によって、剣は一本ごとに切れ味・耐久力・エンチャントの配分と品質が変わります。打ち上がったばかりの剣は真価が分からず、鑑定を進めることで段階的に明らかになります。

工房の外には、8つの町と、魔物の巣と、争う3つの国があります。市場と戦争と魔物は、プレイヤーの操作とは関わりなく毎日動きます。隊商が街道を通って素材を運び、巣が荒れれば荷は細り、戦で道が塞がれば隊商はそもそも来ません。そしてその世界を動かせるのもまた、あなたが打った剣だけです。冒険者が巣を討って帰れば魔物は減り、国の求めに応じて納めた剣はその国の装備となって戦線を押し、町を治める国さえ変えてしまいます。

鑑定を終えた剣は、工房を訪れた冒険者へ託します。冒険者は工房の従業員ではありません。育てることも、呼び戻すこともできず、プレイヤーが関与できるのは彼らが持つ剣だけです。行き先ごとに求められる剣の形は異なり、求めに応えた剣は冒険者を最後まで運び、報酬と名声を積んだログとして返ってきます。力の足りない剣を持たせた冒険者は、二度と工房へ戻りません。その顛末は工房の年表に刻まれ、消えることはありません。

開発者

Nulpoyo（ぬるぽよ）。日本の会社員。本業のかたわら、個人でゲームを制作しています。

放置ゲームが好きで、画面の右下でゲームが動いているだけで作業のモチベーションが上がります。その感覚そのものを形にしたくて作りました。

命の重みを大きくしたのは、そのほうが渡す剣が輝くからです。自分の打った剣が冒険者の命運を握ると思えば、作るほうにも力が入ります。耐久力0の剣を渡して、冒険の途中で「あ、壊れちゃった」となる場面を想像するのも、単純に楽しそうでした。

作り手としても遊び手としても「やり込めるゲーム」が好きで、放っておくと難易度が高くなりがちです。『Desktop Forge』でも、デスクトップの片隅で眺めていられる手触りと、数字を詰めていく余地の両立を狙って、バランス調整に最も時間をかけています。

自分だけの職人の組み合わせを見つけて、最強の一本を打ってみてください。そしてその剣を冒険者に託し、どこまで深く潜れるか試してみてほしいです。

About

Desktop Forge is an idle simulation that runs in a small window at the edge of your desktop while you work. You watch autonomous artisans forge swords, hand those swords to the adventurers who visit, and send them out into the world.

Every artisan has more than a set of stats: over 200 traits, plus their own specialties, likes and dislikes. You never direct them move by move — you decide which jobs each of them is allowed to take, and they choose when to gather, process, forge or rest. Who you put on which stage shapes the flow of the workshop and the swords that come out of it. Everyday interactions make moods rise and fall, and those moods show in the work and in the blades.

The materials used, the artisans involved, their moods and the quality of their work shape how each sword splits between edge, durability and enchantment, and how well it is made. A newly forged blade hides its true nature; appraisal uncovers it step by step.

Beyond your walls lie eight towns, the monster dens beside them, and three nations at war. Markets, war and monsters move every day, whether or not you are watching: caravans carry materials down the roads, a restless den thins out the loads, and fighting can cut a road so no caravan comes at all. And the only thing that moves that world is, again, the sword you forged. Every den your adventurers clear thins out the monsters; blades delivered to a nation's commission become its equipment, push the front line, and can change which nation rules a town.

Appraised swords go to the adventurers who visit your workshop. They are not your employees — you cannot train them, command them, or bring them home. The only thing you control is the sword in their hands. Every destination asks for a different shape of sword: a blade that answers what the road demands carries its bearer through to the end and comes back as a log full of coin and renown. An adventurer sent out with a blade that isn't good enough never returns at all. What happened is written into your workshop's chronicle, and it stays there.

Key features

  • A desktop companion — a compact workshop that keeps moving in the corner of your screen
  • Autonomous artisans — set the work policy and watch an idle workshop run itself
  • Over 200 traits — specialties, likes, dislikes and the mood of the day shape the work
  • A different sword every time — materials, artisans, emotions and luck show in its stats and quality
  • A life on every blade — a weak sword breaks, its bearer never returns, and only the record remains
  • A world that keeps moving — eight towns, monster dens and three warring nations move on without you
  • Swords that change the world — clearing dens calms them; delivered blades push front lines and change who rules
  • Long-term goals — codex records, a workshop expansion tree, a chronicle, and Steam leaderboards

Developer

Nulpoyo. A solo developer based in Japan, making games alongside a full-time office job.

I love idle games. Just having one running in the bottom-right corner of my screen makes me want to keep working. I wanted to build that feeling itself.

I made death matter because it makes the sword you hand over matter. If the blade you forged decides whether an adventurer comes home, you put more into forging it. And picturing someone setting out with a zero-durability sword and going "oh — it broke" halfway through the dungeon simply sounded fun to me.

I like games with a lot to chew on — which means mine tend to end up harder than I intended. Most of the work on Desktop Forge has gone into balancing: keeping it something you can leave running at the edge of your screen, while still leaving numbers worth pushing on.

Find your own combination of smiths and forge the strongest blade you can. Then hand it to an adventurer and see how deep they get.
